Deep Dive into Insider Trading (Suspected Private Sale)

Premise: The official announcement had announced an opening price of $0.01 (the pool's initial price was also actually set at 0.01 USDT, and this point matches the on-chain data). However, the price at which general participants could first buy was about $0.076. This page organizes, based on measured data, “what happened,” “whether there is a problem,” and “who is responsible.” It is a summary as an on-chain analysis, not a legal judgment.

2. Was It a “Private Sale”?

No trace of a typical private sale (a form in which the operator sells directly off-market to a specific counterparty) has been found on-chain. What actually happened was simpler than that: “just before the publicly announced start time, related parties bought first in the public pool.” However, the economic reality is almost the same as a private sale:

PerspectiveContent
Who was able to buy cheaplyOnly the related parties who appear to have known the listing time (the related wallets of validator X)
Difference from the general publicAgainst an average acquisition unit price of about $0.024 for the related parties, the general opening price was $0.076 (about 3.2 times). Only the first few trades at the start of the related parties' buying were able to buy at 0.01
Scale of the amountInvested 1,490 USDT → a market value of about $4,700 at the time of listing → about $26,000 as of 17 (on a valuation basis). Part of it was later sold, and 3,500 USDT was recovered by A8 alone
Form of concealmentThe trades themselves remain on-chain, but because the buyer was not in the name of the validator (self), but was a “related wallet,” the structure was such that, at a glance, it was not recognizable as buying by a related party. The same applies to the LP distributed in advance

3. Is There a Problem?

The core of the problem is 1 thing: a “large-scale advance buying by related parties” that is substantially the same as a private sale. The issues are organized separately:

#Issue
1Large-scale advance buying (the main issue): In the 1 hour just before the publicly announced start time, validator X's related wallets acquired 56.6% of the pool's [42] inventory. By the time the general public became able to participate, the price increase from 0.01 to 0.076 had already become the property of the related parties
2The delay in the start announcement expanded the damage: While the LP addition and the opening price of $0.01 had been announced in advance, the “trading start” announcement (X) was at 15:00 — 1 hour after the actual start. At that point the price was 0.2962 (about 30 times the opening price). General participants who came after seeing the announcement could not buy at 0.01, or even at 0.076
3The buyer was hard to see: The buying was carried out not in the name of the validator (self) but in related wallets, in a structure in which it was not recognizable at a glance as buying by a related party. The advance buying itself has also not been disclosed after the fact
Out of scopeThe distribution of materials for the initial LP is not treated as a problem: In a community-led project, distributing materials for initial liquidity to validators and others is itself within the scope of a legitimate community distribution, and the LP addition had also been announced. In the initial version of this page it was included among the issues, but this is corrected and it is treated as out of scope

Explanations that could stand as a rebuttal are “0.01 was merely the pool's initial set price, and in an AMM it is natural for the price to rise if you buy” and “it was an act of preparing liquidity.” However, if it were liquidity preparation, the natural form would be to add LP in a wallet in the operator's name, and what was actually carried out was a concentrated sweep of spot buying by related wallets in the 1 hour just before listing, which does not match this explanation. Also, the point that 1,490 USDT was issued to AF on the same day as the buying of [42] also suggests that it was planned.

Reservation regarding the legal positioning: Whether a token qualifies as a security and which country's regulations apply depend on the jurisdiction and the individual circumstances, so this page does not definitively state that “it is illegal.” What can be stated definitively is the problem of facts and fairness: the divergence between the announcement and the reality, advance trading based on undisclosed information, and the lack of disclosure.

5. What Is Still Not Known

• The route by which the listing time was passed to the X side (whether it was shared by the operator, or whether it was a person inside the operator) cannot be proved on-chain.
• Who instructed the issuance of 1,490 USDT is undetermined.
• The wording of the official announcement itself (the possibility that the definition of “opening price” referred to the pool's initial price) is off-chain information, and on this page it is based on the premise presented by the user.
